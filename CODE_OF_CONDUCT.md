# Code of conduct

## Our standard

Harnessing 101 welcomes technical disagreement and expects professional conduct. Participants must discuss the work on its merits, state uncertainty plainly, and give others enough context to respond. Feedback should address behavior, evidence, design, or impact rather than a person's identity or presumed intent.

Examples of constructive conduct include:

- asking for evidence without belittling the contributor;
- explaining the effect of a requested change;
- acknowledging mistakes and correcting the record;
- respecting privacy, confidentiality, and security boundaries; and
- making space for contributors with different experience levels and communication styles.

Unacceptable conduct includes harassment, discrimination, threats, sexualized attention, deliberate intimidation, sustained disruption, publication of private information, impersonation, or retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This code applies in project repositories, reviews, issue trackers, community spaces, and private project communication. It also applies when someone formally represents the project in another venue.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to **rafael.ca.dev@gmail.com**. Do not open a public issue when a report identifies a person, contains private information, or could expose someone to retaliation.

Include the relevant dates, locations, participants, what happened, and any records the reporter is comfortable sharing. Reports will be limited to people needed to assess and act on them. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised when safety or law requires disclosure, but unnecessary disclosure is not acceptable.

If the named contact is involved in the report, do not send it to that person; ask another project maintainer to receive it. The owner must replace the placeholder above with a route that supports this fallback before public contributions open.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may clarify expectations, request a correction or apology, remove content, limit participation, temporarily suspend access, or permanently exclude a participant. The response should be proportionate to severity, impact, pattern, and risk of recurrence. Immediate restrictions may be used when needed to protect participants or the project.

Enforcement decisions should record the conduct at issue, the policy applied, the action, and any review condition. Reports made in good faith will not be punished merely because available evidence does not establish a violation. Deliberately false or retaliatory reports are themselves misconduct.
